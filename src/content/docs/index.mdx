	<section id="about" className="course-section">
		<h2>About</h2>
		<p>{'Modern robot policies are often presented as a model and a benchmark score. On a physical robot, the result also depends on the data collection setup, robot interface, training pipeline, inference loop, and evaluation protocol. We will study these pieces together using the SO-101 arm and the LeRobot software stack.'}</p>
		<p>{'The course begins with a short essentials recap and data collection, moves through single-task imitation learning, and then studies generalist robot policies and current research directions. Most assignments will be completed in small teams. There are no exams.'}</p>
	</section>

	<section id="goals" className="course-section">
		<h2>Course goals</h2>
		<p>By the end of the course, students should be able to:</p>
		<ul className="course-goals">
			<li>Describe the modern landscape of data-driven robot learning and the common system designs used in robot learning.</li>
			<li>Assemble, calibrate, and safely operate a robotic manipulator built from readily available electromechanical components.</li>
			<li>Implement, train, and evaluate modern imitation learning approaches for simple manipulation tasks.</li>
			<li>Deploy a vision-language-action (VLA) model on a physical robot and characterize its success and failure modes.</li>
			<li>Compare the tradeoffs between policy architectures, low-level design choices, and data collection methods.</li>
			<li>Define evaluation metrics and failure taxonomies, and conduct reproducible robot experiments.</li>
			<li>For graduate students: complete an open-ended mini research project in robot learning.</li>
		</ul>
	</section>
