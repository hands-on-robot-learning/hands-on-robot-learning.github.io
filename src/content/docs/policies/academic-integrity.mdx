title: Academic integrity
description: Collaboration rules, disclosure expectations, and what not to do.
---

Students must follow **Johns Hopkins academic integrity policies**.

This course includes group work, open-source software, online documentation, and the use of LLMs/coding assistants. We want you to collaborate productively while being honest about what you did and what you used.

## Collaboration

**Within teams:** collaboration is encouraged.

**Across teams:** you may discuss:

- Assignment specifications
- Hardware issues
- Setup problems
- High-level debugging ideas
- General lecture concepts
- Public documentation
- Non-solution-specific advice

Across teams, you may **not**:

- Copy private solution code
- Submit another team’s dataset as your own
- Submit another team’s checkpoint as your own
- Share hidden evaluation positions/details if held back
- Misrepresent who did the work

## Required disclosure and citation

You must cite/disclose when you use:

- Public repositories
- Starter code
- Pretrained models
- External datasets
- LLMs/coding assistants
- Copied/adapted code (even if modified)

Disclosures should be short and specific (what you used and how you used it).

## Examples of academic dishonesty

- Falsifying success rates
- Editing videos to hide failures
- Cherry-picking official trials
- Manual intervention reported as autonomous
- Misrepresenting dataset/model details
- Submitting code/results the team cannot explain
- Copying private team work
- Hiding external assistance when disclosure is required

If you are unsure what is appropriate, ask early.
