Participation means contributing to a lab-style course community: asking good questions, sharing what you learn while debugging, and helping the class operate smoothly on shared hardware.

## Examples

- Asking useful questions
- Sharing debugging observations
- Participating in robot mystery discussions
- Team check-ins
- Peer feedback
- Responsible shared-hardware usage
- Bug reports/documentation fixes
- Paper/system lightning talk

## Robot Mystery of the Week

Most weeks start with a short “Robot Mystery of the Week”: a failure clip or behavior to diagnose (perception vs calibration vs control vs policy).

Good participation includes:

- Proposing plausible hypotheses
- Suggesting minimal tests to distinguish causes
- Sharing “what we tried” and “what changed”

## Paper and system lightning talks

At least once during the semester, each team will contribute a short lightning talk (paper or system). The goal is to share high-signal ideas and practical takeaways that help others build and debug.

## Engineering citizenship

Engineering citizenship is the set of behaviors that keep a shared robotics lab running:

- Leaving hardware in good condition for the next team
- Writing down issues early (with evidence)
- Communicating constraints (hardware access, compute, time) before deadlines
- Helping unblock others without crossing into inappropriate solution-sharing
